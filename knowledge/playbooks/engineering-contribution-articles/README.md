# Engineering contribution articles

Explain the contribution without claiming the whole project or more than the
results establish. The article carries the evidence; its share text gives
readers a reason to open it.

## Choose the deliverable

Distinguish a full article, the feed text accompanying a native article, and a
standalone social post. Use the stated destination; clarify only when ambiguous.
Carry forward requested voice, length, FAQ, conclusion, and acknowledgements.
These are task requirements, not defaults for every article.

Customer success stories and portfolio conversion pages belong to
[case-study-pages](../case-study-pages/README.md). This procedure owns
first-person engineering contribution write-ups, not benchmark design.

## Calibrate the comparison

- Resolve identities before comparing. A publisher's blog and the project shipping
  its feature may describe one implementation, not competing products. Check the
  primary source and identify the version actually inspected.
- Separate implementation improvements from measured outcomes. A clearer handoff
  or stronger verification can be valuable without proving greater speed, lower
  cost, or general superiority.
- Describe the evidence's unit and limits. Repeating one workload is not testing
  many workloads. A passing artifact does not prove the whole workflow completed.
  Distinguish product failures, harness failures, and incomplete recordings.
- Compare compatible measurements. Do not present differently scoped pilots as
  matched benchmarks, or a budget constraint as evidence of performance.
  Preserve failures and qualifications when shortening the story.

## Credit the contribution

Separate the existing project, upstream inspiration, and the author's specific
work. Thank the relevant person for the opportunity to contribute without
claiming project ownership or adding defensive disclaimers. Do not invent names,
roles, or original authorship. Keep source links with the article's claims.

Keep requested acknowledgements when shortening or changing channels. Names are
not handles; use a verified account or plain name rather than guessing a tag.

## Keep the author's voice

Use [house-writing-style](../house-writing-style/README.md) for prose review.
Honor preferences such as no em dashes or fewer colons without altering
quotations, URLs, or code. For feedback like "sounds AI-written," rethink the
angle and cadence rather than only swapping words. A concrete premise can be a
better hook than a generic question or polished metaphor; judge it in context.

Keep humour close to the user's phrasing rather than forcing a technical pun.
A budget joke does not replace honest testing limits. Add substance, not
padding, to meet a requested article length. When asked to change only the hook,
revise that part rather than adding another summary.

## Adapt the share text

An attached LinkedIn article needs a short personal introduction, not a second
article. Lead with the premise, add the relevant acknowledgement, and omit an
unneeded link placeholder. For current publishing fields, consult
[LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a522427/publish-articles-on-linkedin).
Brevity here is editorial advice, not a platform rule.

A standalone X post needs enough context to explain the feature and why someone
should read more. Repeating the core idea is useful; do not carry over a
minimal LinkedIn caption blindly. Include the article link and retain credit.

An optional P.S. can explain why the full article is hosted elsewhere when the
user requests it. Treat account limitations as task context, not a permanent
personal fact.

## Verify the result

Review claims against sources, contributor wording against known roles, and the
post in its feed context. Check article length and sections on the saved file.
Count the complete social post against the confirmed account limit, including
credit, handles, platform-weighted links, and any P.S. Recheck after additions.
Confirm approved details survived revisions. Report missing evidence without
inventing it or implying publication occurred.
