# Interpret a reference before choosing a construction technique

Use for reference-led logo, lettering or illustration changes whose desired
visual effect is unclear. A reference can contain several different design ideas;
liking the example does not identify which one should transfer.

## Find the decision that changes the work

1. **Separate the visual roles.** Identify the focal mark, supporting lettering,
   their relationship and the presentation. Describe concrete features such as
   shared strokes, negative space, silhouette or optical spacing. A custom
   interlocking symbol beside restrained type offers two different lessons;
   reducing both to “clean, bold, black and white” loses that distinction.
2. **Name the intended transfer.** State which feature or relationship the draft
   should demonstrate, what stays protected, and what is merely presentation.
   Keep visible evidence separate from inferred preference. Permission to explore
   a cleaner direction is not approval of a particular font or construction.
3. **Resolve a consequential fork.** If two readings would produce substantially
   different work, ask which part of the example matters, or compare small
   authorized concept sketches. Ask about that difference, not a style menu whose
   options both omit it. An explicit request for only the supporting typography
   is enough to proceed; do not force a monogram or another clarification round.
4. **Test the idea before expanding it.** Make the smallest useful visual proof
   of the intended relationship before producing a family of variants or polished
   exports. Compare it with the reference at the intended size: can the proposed
   connection, silhouette or spacing actually be seen, or did only the font weight
   and background change? Tools and licensed fonts are means, not substitutes for
   this decision. Font-based wordmarks are valid when they satisfy the brief.

## Review fit separately from fidelity

Check the visual proof against the stated design goal as well as the protected
artwork. Spelling, non-overlap, editable paths and successful delivery establish
technical correctness, not aesthetic acceptance. Keep the draft available for
review without promoting it to the approved baseline.

A rejection establishes that the candidate failed, not why. Revisit the reference
and stated goal; treat a proposed explanation or replacement direction as a
hypothesis until supported. Do not turn an unanswered suggestion into a user
preference, and do not ban an entire technique because one treatment was rejected.
