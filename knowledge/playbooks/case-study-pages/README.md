# Case-study pages

A case-study page proves a transformation relevant to the next buyer. The
customer is the protagonist; the offering helps them make progress. Optimize for
the reader's decision, not an internal victory lap.

## Boundaries

Use this for standalone customer stories and portfolio project pages built from
real outcomes. Use `../copywriting/README.md` when the wider offer, positioning,
or conversion path is unsettled. Hand visual composition to the design owner
after the evidence and page jobs are clear.

Never invent a customer, quote, metric, timeline, obstacle, approval, product
role, or causal claim. Mark unsupported material as
`[PLACEHOLDER: evidence needed]`.

## Workflow

1. Set the deployment brief: business goal, target reader, relevant offer or
   capability, arrival context, page job, primary CTA, and intended reuse.
2. Choose a story that resembles the desired buyer or situation and contains a
   result that supports that goal. Fame and impressive numbers do not compensate
   for poor relevance.
3. Build an evidence ledger for quotes, dates, baselines, results, timeframes,
   product actions, alternatives, friction, artifacts, and approval status. Load
   `references/evidence-capture.md` when the source material is thin.
4. Find the causal spine: **trigger -> stakes -> decision -> approach -> change
   -> meaning**. Separate what happened from what the offering demonstrably
   influenced.
5. Draft in page order, then tighten the scan path and the full narrative.
6. Size the page for reader context and proof burden, not a preset word count.
   Put concise orientation and strongest proof near the top; keep useful detail
   below for readers evaluating a risky or expensive decision.

## Page architecture

Use labels that fit the brand; this is an argument, not a rigid template.

- **Hero:** recognizable customer or situation plus a verified result or urgent
  pain; add a concise context line and matched quote when available.
- **Proof snapshot:** two to four outcomes with baseline, timeframe, scope, or
  other qualification close enough to interpret them correctly.
- **Challenge:** enter at the triggering event, establish the old state and
  stakes, and surface pains the target reader recognizes.
- **Decision and approach:** explain why this route was chosen, what was done,
  why key choices mattered, and any honest adjustment or tradeoff. Tie every
  named feature or deliverable to a customer action and consequence.
- **Results and meaning:** combine verified quantitative or observable change
  with operational, business, or human significance. Do not dump metrics without
  explaining why they mattered.
- **Next step:** connect the same challenge or desired outcome to a logical CTA
  for this reader's funnel stage.

Use real logos, portraits, screenshots, artifacts, and before/after evidence
only when available and approved; list missing assets rather than creating
decorative proof.

## Deliverable

Return the brief, evidence gaps, publishable copy in page order, and separate
proof/asset requests. Add testimonial snippets or other reuse variants only when
requested, derived from the approved source story.

## Verification

- Every decision-driving claim maps to the evidence ledger and carries needed
  qualification.
- Attributed quotes preserve the speaker's meaning and approved wording.
- The target reader can scan the customer, challenge, approach, result, and next
  step without reading every paragraph.
- The story explains causality without overstating attribution.
- The customer's approval covers the final copy and identifiable assets.
