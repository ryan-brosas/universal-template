# Capture case-study evidence

Load this reference when selecting a customer, requesting participation,
interviewing, or turning scattered source material into page-ready proof.

## Select for strategic fit

Start with customers who are positive, available, and safe to feature, then rank
for the actual page goal:

- similarity to the target buyer, role, industry, situation, or use case;
- a specific challenge the target reader recognizes;
- a verified result or meaningful observable change;
- enough detail to explain why the change happened;
- permission to publish the identity and useful proof assets.

High satisfaction scores, detailed reviews, support wins, sales notes, and
account-team recommendations can surface candidates. They do not replace
consent or evidence.

## Make a low-friction request

A participation request should state:

- why this customer's story is relevant and what you hope to feature;
- the real time commitment and interview format;
- what identity, numbers, screenshots, or internal details may appear;
- that nothing is published without their review and approval;
- the legitimate benefit to them, such as visibility or a useful story asset;
- the proposed review and publication timeline.

Do not promise anonymity, exposure, timing, or benefits the team cannot provide.

## Prepare the interview

Prefer one primary storyteller; add a second only when they supply a distinct
part of the decision or result. Send topic areas early, request missing metrics
in advance, test the recording setup, obtain recording consent, and keep a
backup. Listen more than you speak. Ask for a concrete example, then probe
"why?", "what changed?", and "how do you know?" until the answer is usable.

## Interview from before through after

Ask open questions about real events, then use closed follow-ups for exact facts.

### Before

- What was happening when the old approach became unacceptable?
- Walk me through the last time the problem occurred.
- What did it cost in time, money, risk, missed work, or confidence?
- What had you tried, and why was it not enough?
- What outcome were you trying to reach?

### Decision and implementation

- Which alternatives did you consider? What made this route fit?
- What nearly stopped the decision?
- What happened first, then next?
- Which feature, service, or choice mattered most, and why?
- What was difficult or changed along the way? How was it handled?

### After

- What changed first? What changed later?
- Which results can you verify? Compared with what baseline and timeframe?
- What did the result make possible for the team, business, or individual?
- What surprised you?
- What would you tell someone in the same situation?

Follow up for dates, denominators, screenshots, reports, and wording the speaker
could not confirm live.

## Build the evidence ledger

Record one row per usable item:

| Fact or quote | Source | Verification | Scope/qualifier | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Distinguish customer-reported outcomes from independently verified results.
Preserve recordings and transcripts according to the project's privacy and
retention rules. Before publication, send the final copy and identifiable assets
for explicit approval and record requested redactions or restrictions.
