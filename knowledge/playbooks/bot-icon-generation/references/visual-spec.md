# Minimal bot icon visual specification

## Identity from the target

Read the target's skin tone or facial surface base color, hair color and silhouette,
length, parting, curliness, bangs and ponytail style. Preserve base colors and at
most three core identifying features. Prioritize the large silhouette over detail;
retain characteristic ears, hats, glasses, beards, decorations or mechanical parts
only as needed for recognition. Simplify clothing and equipment to essential parts.

Do not standardize skin or hair to a preferred shade. Keep absent or obscured hair
absent or obscured; add no invented bangs, accessories or mechanical parts. Do not
copy the original expression or realistic facial structure. A separate style
reference contributes no skin color, hair color, hairstyle or decorations.

## Face

Use a large round bot face with simplified hair and identifying features. Express
cuteness through rounded proportions and tilt, not expression decorations. Render
the original skin/surface color in broad smooth areas, with cheeks and chin joined
smoothly: no pointed/angular jaw or realistic skeletal anatomy.

No mouth or nose, including smiling, dot-shaped or cat mouths. Add a small, light,
low-saturation oval blush on each cheek, harmonized with the skin tone: flat color,
no lines or shimmer. Leave ample empty facial space around the eyes and blush.
Key glasses or a beard may remain minimal; glasses cannot obscure eyes, and a beard
cannot depict a mouth or realistic facial structure.

## Eyes — highest priority

Draw exactly two solid, nearly black capsules, identical in size and parallel.
Upright, they are vertical rounded-end bars with height about 2.5–3 times width.
Each long axis is perpendicular to the line joining the eye centers. Rotate this
whole arrangement with the head; never turn them into horizontal bars or closed eyes.

Fill each capsule with one uniform color. The capsule itself is the eye: no separate
eyeball, pupil, iris, eye white, reflection, sparkle, gradient, eyelash, eyelid,
eyebrow or decorative border. No hook like the numeral 1 or base stroke.

## Composition

Use a 1:1 square canvas with one character, peeking from the bottom-left corner.
Enlarge the face, hair or head outline to fill most of the canvas. Rotate the head
approximately 15–20 degrees clockwise: the viewer-left eye sits slightly higher
than the viewer-right eye. Face, both eyes, hair and attached decorations share
that tilt.

Crop the head naturally at the left and bottom canvas edges. The chin touches or
partly crosses the bottom; leave dark background space at the upper right. Both
eyes remain fully visible and unobscured. Avoid centered, symmetrical passport
framing or a small complete head. No torso or hands; allow only a small neck/collar
fragment at the bottom if essential for identification. Enlargement and cropping
are intentional.

## Hair, details and color

Reduce hair to a few large smooth masses, preserving the bangs' flow, sense of
length and overall silhouette; omit fine strands and stray hairs. Use soft flat
representative colors and almost no outlines. Distinguish adjacent shapes through
color fields, not thick black borders. Allow only broad, faint shading about one
step from the base color; optional hair highlights occupy one or two large fields.

Decorations and machinery retain only silhouettes and major connections. Omit
small screws, wiring, circuits, dense panel lines and complex patterns. At thumbnail
size, both black capsule eyes and the core silhouette must read immediately.

## Background and exclusions

Use solid dark charcoal across the entire background. No background objects or
patterns, circular frames, badge borders, text, numbers, logos, watermarks, speech
bubbles, exclamation marks, glitter, particles, light flares or lens flares. Avoid
photorealism, 3D rendering, oil-paint texture, rough sketch lines, excessive gloss,
complex shading and dense decoration.

## Collision priority

Resolve conflicts in this order:

1. Two plain black capsule eyes.
2. Round bot face without mouth or nose.
3. Tilted close-up with both eyes fully visible.
4. The target's base colors and key identifying features.
5. Other details.

If bangs obscure eyes, adjust their length, width or placement while preserving
the main flow. If cropping would entirely remove a key decoration, adjust its size
or position just enough to leave recognizable parts. Neither adjustment authorizes
inventing features absent from the target.
